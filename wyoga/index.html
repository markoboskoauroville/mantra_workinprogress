Feature suggestion: Wyoga Voice Coach (paid upgrade)

Idea: a paid upgrade with a voice and an AI server. While you hold a pose, the app speaks to you

and corrects you limb by limb: "lift your left arm a little", "your right leg is not straight".

The voice comes from the Speechify API.

How it should be built so it is fast and cheap:

  • The checking stays on the phone. The pose model already gives the joints (shoulders, elbows, hips, knees, ankles) many times a second. Each asana gets target angles (for example Warrior II: front knee about 90°, arms level at 180°). The app compares the live angles with the targets and finds the worst limb. No server round trip per frame, so the advice comes within a second.
  • The voice is pre-rendered, not generated live. A fixed set of short phrases ("lift your left arm", "straighten your right leg", "hips higher", "good, hold it") is rendered once with the Speechify API, stored as small audio files, and played instantly. That costs a few cents once instead of per user.
  • The AI server does the coaching on top. After a session (or every few seconds), the angle summary goes to an AI model on the server, which writes a personal sentence ("your Tree is steadier than yesterday; press the foot into the thigh") and Speechify speaks it. This is the part that justifies the paid tier.
  • Paid upgrade. Web: Stripe. Android from Google Play: digital subscriptions must go through Google Play Billing; a TWA can use it through the Digital Goods API (Bubblewrap supports it).
  • Keys stay on the server. The Speechify and AI keys never go into the web page; the page calls Svemir's own API route (a Next.js route handler on Vercel), which calls Speechify and the AI.

Prompt for v0: the pose correction algorithm

This is the engine under the Voice Coach, asked of v0 on its own so it can be built and tested first, with text

feedback on screen; the voice is added on top later.

Build a pose correction algorithm into Wyoga's practice screen. It must run entirely in the browser on the pose landmarks the app already gets from the camera (no server call per frame). Do not change the design or the existing pose check that stops the alarm.

1. Landmarks and angles. From the pose model's landmarks (shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles, plus nose) compute every frame:
   - joint angles: left/right elbow, shoulder (arm vs torso), hip (torso vs thigh), knee;
   - segment angles against the vertical/horizontal: torso tilt, each arm's elevation, shoulder line and hip line tilt;
   - use only landmarks with visibility > 0.6; if a needed one is missing, say "Step back so your whole body is in view" instead of a correction.
   Smooth each value with a moving average over about 300 ms so jitter does not trigger advice. Detect which side faces the camera for side-on poses (Down Dog) and mirror left/right correctly for the front camera, so "left arm" always means the user's own left arm.

2. Pose definitions as data. One config object per asana (Upward Salute, Down Dog, Warrior II, Tree, each step of Sun Salutation), each rule with: id, the measured angle, target, tolerance, priority, and the instruction text. Examples:
   - Upward Salute: both elbows 170-180°, both arms elevated 160-180°, torso vertical within 10°.
   - Warrior II: front knee 80-100° and over the ankle, back leg straight 165-180°, arms horizontal within 10°, torso vertical within 10°, hips square to the camera.
   - Tree: standing knee 170-180°, raised foot at or above the other knee, hips level within 8°, palms together in front of the chest or overhead.
   - Down Dog: hips the highest point, arms straight 165-180°, legs straight 160-180°, hip angle 60-100°.
   Put tolerances in one place so they are easy to tune.

3. Choosing the correction. Each frame, score every rule (how far outside its tolerance, times its priority). Pick the single worst rule. Only give a correction when it has been the worst for at least 1 second, and at most one new correction every 3 seconds; never repeat the same one within 6 seconds. Phrase it as a direction of movement, short and concrete, with the body part and side: "Lift your left arm a little higher", "Straighten your right leg", "Bend your front knee more", "Level your hips", "Lift your hips higher". When every rule is inside tolerance, show "Good, hold it" and start the hold timer; a break longer than 0.5 s resets the hold.

4. On screen. Show the current correction as a large text line over the camera picture, and colour the skeleton: the limb that needs correcting in orange, correct limbs in green. Add a small accuracy score 0-100 for the current pose.

5. Session summary. When practice ends, store: asana, time held, the corrections given (counts), and best and worst value for each rule. Keep it locally, and expose it as a JSON object, because a later paid AI coach will read it.

6. Hooks for voice. Every correction emits an event with a stable id (e.g. LEFT_ARM_HIGHER, RIGHT_LEG_STRAIGHTEN, FRONT_KNEE_BEND, HIPS_LEVEL, HIPS_HIGHER, HOLD_GOOD, STEP_BACK) plus its text, so a voice layer can later play a pre-recorded phrase for each id.

7. A debug view (hidden behind a long-press on the title): live angles, the active rule and its distance from target, frame rate. Make sure it runs at a steady frame rate on a mid-range Android phone (process the pose at 15-20 fps if needed while the video stays smooth).

When done, explain where the pose definitions live and how to add a new asana, and how I can test each correction.

Idea for Svemir: more cameras, better skeleton

One camera sees the body flat. It has to guess how far an arm or a leg points towards it or away, so a raised arm

pointing at the camera, or a hand behind the back, is often wrong. With two or more cameras looking from different

angles (for example the phone in front and a second phone or a laptop at the side), each joint is seen twice. Knowing

where the cameras stand, the app calculates the joint's real position in space (triangulation), the same way our two

eyes see depth. The farther apart the cameras (ideally about 90°), the better the depth.

What it takes:

  • Calibration once: each camera's lens (focal length and distortion, important for wide lenses) and where the cameras stand relative to each other. The usual way: the user shows a printed checkerboard (ChArUco board) to all cameras for a few seconds; the app works it out.
  • Time sync: frames from both cameras matched by timestamp (a clap or a flash at the start also works).
  • Connection: the second phone joins the session by a QR code and sends its pose points (not video) over the local Wi-Fi or WebRTC, so it is light.
  • The cameras do not need to be the same model or lens; each one is calibrated on its own.

The free open-source FreeMoCap project does exactly this with ordinary webcams and is a good reference. For Wyoga it

could be a Premium "Pro tracking" mode: two phones, much more precise corrections, especially for side-on poses like

Down Dog and Warrior II.

Add an optional "Two cameras" mode to Wyoga (Premium). A second phone joins the practice session by scanning a QR code shown on the first phone; both run the pose model locally and stream only pose landmarks with timestamps (WebRTC data channel, or a small relay) to the main phone. Calibration: a one-minute wizard where the user holds a printed ChArUco board (offer a PDF) visible to both cameras; compute each camera's intrinsics and the relative pose. Then triangulate each landmark from both views (weighted by confidence, fall back to one view when a joint is seen by one camera only), smooth, and feed the 3D skeleton to the matching and correction logic in place of the single-camera estimate. Show both camera feeds side by side with the skeleton, and a "tracking quality" indicator.

Pose detection: what each platform has

A pose engine is a neural network that finds the body's joints in each camera frame; the skeleton is the lines between them. Some systems have one built in, others need the app to bring its own.

EngineWherePoints3DNotes
Apple Vision (built into the system)iPhone, iPad, Mac (since iOS 14 / macOS 11, 2020)19 (ankles and wrists are the ends: no heels, toes or fingers)Yes, 17 joints in metres, since iOS 17 / macOS 14 (2023)Free, on the device, very fast on Apple's chip, several people. Apple only.
Google MediaPipe Pose Landmarker (BlazePose)Android, iPhone, web browser, desktop33 (incl. heels, toe tips, index fingers, thumbs, pinkies)Yes, "world" coordinates in metresFree, on the device, lite/full/heavy models (speed vs precision), optional body mask. The same engine everywhere.

Android itself---No pose engine in the system: every app brings MediaPipe or ML Kit.

For Wyoga: MediaPipe Pose Landmarker is the best fit: it runs in the browser (your web app and the Android wrapper), on a native Android app and on iPhone, so the angles and the corrections are the same everywhere. Its 33 points include the heels and toes, which matter for yoga (Tree: the foot on the thigh; Warrior: the front foot turned out; Down Dog: heels to the floor). Use the full model on phones and heavy on computers. Apple Vision is excellent if one day you make a native iPhone app, but it has no feet or hand points, so it is better as a second opinion than as the main engine.

Precision, in practice: all of them are good in a frontal view with the whole body visible and good light. They all struggle with a limb pointing at the camera, a hand behind the back, and side-on poses where one leg hides the other: that is where two cameras help most (see above).
